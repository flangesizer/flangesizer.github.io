Breast pump flange size guide

Most breast pumps ship with 24 mm or 28 mm flanges (breast shields), which are larger than many nipples. Here is how to measure at home, find a starting flange size to try, and check the fit while pumping.

How to measure your nipple at home

Whichever method you use, a few small habits make the number more useful:

  • Measure before pumping (or before a feed), because nipples often swell during pumping.
  • Measure the nipple only, not the areola — straight across the base where the nipple meets the areola.
  • Measure both sides separately. It is very common for the left and right sides to need different sizes.
  • Re-check over time. Nipple size can change from late pregnancy to early postpartum and later months of pumping.

Option 1: With a millimeter ruler

  1. 1Stand in front of a mirror with a ruler marked in millimeters (mm), or gently stimulate the nipple so it is upright.
  2. 2Line up the zero mark with one side of the nipple base and read across to the other side, leaving out the areola.
  3. 3Write down the number in mm for each side, then look it up in the size chart below.

If you have a circle-hole sizing ruler, pick the smallest hole your nipple slips through without the sides touching the edges.

Option 2: With one photo and any standard-size card

Reading a few millimeters on a ruler while holding it yourself can be awkward. Any standard-size card — such as a gift card, loyalty card or hotel key — is always 85.6 × 54 mm, so it can act as a ruler in a photo.

  1. 1Turn the card face down (a plain side works best; only the card’s outline is used) and rest your nipple on it near an edge.
  2. 2Hold the card flat by its far end so your fingers stay away from the corners, and keep all four corners in the frame.
  3. 3Hold your phone straight above, parallel to the card, about 20–30 cm (8–12 in) away in even light with no glare, and take one photo.

The free Flange Sizer app measures your nipple from that photo on your phone. Nothing is uploaded or saved, and it works in airplane mode. You can also type in a ruler measurement directly.

Between whole millimeters? Add 2 to 4 mm to your measurement and pick a listed size inside that range (for example, 16.5 mm gives 18.5–20.5 mm, so 19 or 20 mm). If your measurement is below 9 mm or above 34 mm, talk with a lactation consultant (IBCLC) about specialty flanges or inserts.

Left and right don’t match? If one flange size sits inside both sides’ ranges, you can try using that one size for both. Otherwise, use each side’s own size or insert.

About the +2 to +4 mm range (and closer fitting)

Older pump manuals often suggested adding 3 to 4 mm to your nipple width so the nipple has room to expand slightly during pumping. Today, many lactation consultants (IBCLCs) fit closer to the nipple’s actual width — sometimes only 1 to 2 mm larger, or close to the measured width itself — because extra space can pull areola into the tunnel.

That is why any chart gives a starting size to try rather than a final answer. If the starting size pulls in areola or feels loose, try the smaller size in the range (or a step closer to your nipple width). If the tunnel rubs or pinches, try the next size up.

Flange inserts: Most pump brands sell 24 mm or 28 mm flanges, and silicone flange inserts slip inside a 24 mm (or sometimes 28 mm) tunnel to bring it down to smaller sizes like 13–21 mm without buying a full set of new parts.

Signs of a good fit vs. a poor fit

Home measurements are estimates and can be off by a few mm, so always judge the fit by how your nipple looks and feels while pumping.

Signs of a good fit

  • Nipple moves freely back and forth in the tunnel without rubbing the walls.
  • Little areola is pulled in with each pump cycle.
  • No pain, pinching or rubbing during or after pumping, and breasts feel softer afterward.

Signs the flange may be too big

  • A noticeable ring of areola gets pulled deep into the tunnel.
  • Your nipple or areola looks swollen, puffy or creased after pumping.
  • Breasts still feel full or milk output seems lower than expected.

Signs the flange may be too small

  • The sides of your nipple rub or scrape against the tunnel walls.
  • Your nipple looks pinched, red, compressed or turns white after pumping.
  • Pumping feels uncomfortable or leads to sore, cracked or blistered skin.

Pump suction also matters: turning suction higher than is comfortable can make any flange hurt, even when the size is close.

When to see a lactation consultant (IBCLC)

A sizing guide can help you pick a starting size, but watching how your nipple moves in real time tells much more than a static measurement. Stop and talk with an IBCLC lactation consultant, midwife or doctor if:

  • pumping hurts, even after trying nearby sizes or lowering suction;
  • you have cracked, bleeding, bruised or blistering skin, or nipples that turn white or purple;
  • your nipple stretches far down the tunnel (sometimes called elastic tissue) and common sizes still feel uncomfortable;
  • you notice clogged ducts, breast tenderness or ongoing supply concerns.

Not medical advice: This guide and the Flange Sizer app give general information only. Measurements are estimates and can be off by a few mm. They are not medical advice and do not diagnose, treat or monitor any condition. Flange Sizer is for adults (18+) measuring themselves.
